Things that are always true 
Every corner is fair. The walls are rotated four ways around the centre, so each corner faces exactly the same walls. Resources and crates also arrive in fair groups of four. 
You only see nearby things. Your bot sees 3 cells around it, and only where no wall blocks the line. This is called fog of war. 
Some things are always visible: the whole wall layout, the Core, the uplink pads, crates, smoke, airstrike markers, and any marked bot. 
You see your own score and your rank (1st to 4th). You never see another bot's score. 
Dying never takes away points you already scored. It costs you what you were carrying, your killing streak, and a few turns.

03 YO U R T U RN 
Look, decide, act 
Every turn, the game calls one function in your bot: decide(view) . The view is everything your bot can see right now. Your function returns one action. 
ALL FOU R BOTS M OVE AT THE SA M E TI M E 
Every bot decides from the same picture of the board. Then all the actions happen together. You never see what the others chose until the next turn. 
Moving 
Your bot moves one cell per turn, in 8 directions: NORTH , SOUTH , EAST , WEST , NORTHEAST , NORTHWEST , SOUTHEAST , SOUTHWEST . 
Diagonal steps count as one move. So the distance between two cells is the bigger of the two gaps: across or down. Walking into a wall or another bot is allowed, but you do not move. Your energy is still spent. 
Walking off the edge of the board is illegal. 
If two bots try to step onto the same cell, neither moves. 
Nothing can slip diagonally between two walls that touch at a corner. Not a bot, not a shot, not a line of sight. 
Health and energy 
Your bot has 70 health and 50 energy. 
Every action costs energy. You get +5 energy back every turn (+10 in Overtime), up to 50. 
Every action and its cost 
ACTION CODE ENERGY WHAT IT DOES 
Move Action.move("EAST") 1 Step one cell. 
Attack Action.attack(bot_id) 5 Hit a bot next to you, diagonals included, for 16–24 damage. It lands even if the target walks away this turn. 
Shoot Action.shoot("NORTH") 6 Fire in a straight line up to 4 cells. Hits the first bot on the line for 10–14. Walls stop it. It lands after everyone moves.

ILLEGAL ACTIONS CO ST YOU 
An illegal action loses the turn. Examples: not enough energy, a target too far away, a move off the board, an ability still recharging. One illegal action in the admission test gets your bot rejected. So your bot must check every action against the view before it returns it. 
Death and respawn 
At 0 health your bot is killed. It leaves the board and comes back 3 turns later at its own base, with full health and energy. 
Everything it carried spills on the floor as one pile. Anyone can collect it. A held item drops as a crate. Its killing streak goes back to 0. Banked points are never lost. 
Spawn protection: for 2 turns after coming back, your bot takes no damage. Protection ends early if it attacks, shoots, collects, or uses an ability or item. 
Every base sits in a walled pocket with two exits, so nobody can shoot into a spawn from far away. 
Marked bots 
Some bots are marked. Every other bot can see a marked bot anywhere on the board, even through walls. A bot is marked while it: 
MARK WHY

How kills work 
Kill Confirmed. When a bot dies, it drops a tag where it fell. A bot standing on a tag at the end of a turn picks it up. If a killer picks it up, that is confirmed (+15). If anyone else does, even the victim after respawning, that is denied (+10). Tags disappear after 6 turns. 
Shared kills. Every bot that dealt damage on the killing turn gets the kill. 
Streaks. Your streak is the number of kills since you last died. At a 3-kill streak you get a free radar sweep: you see every enemy for 3 turns. At a 5-kill streak you get an airstrike in your item slot. 
The crown. At the start of each turn, the bot with the highest score wears the crown. It is marked for everyone. Nobody learns by how much it leads. 
A bot killed by its own bomb gives nobody any points.
How cargo works 
Collected resources are not points yet. They only score once you stand on your own base. 
Your hold carries 4 resource units. When it is full, you must bank before you can collect again. 
Hot pickup: collecting while another bot is within 2 cells loads the resource at 1.3 times its value. A spilled pile never gets this bonus again. 
Cargo still in your hold when the match ends scores nothing. 
The match starts with 4 resources. Most resources arrive later in supply waves during the match. 
How the Core works 
1. Spawn. The Core appears on turn 8 on one of the four centre cells. 
2. Pick up. Stand on it and collect "CORE" . You are now marked. If you die, the Core drops where you fell. 3. Plant. Step onto any uplink pad while carrying it. It plants itself at the end of that turn. 
4. Uplink. If it survives 3 turns, the planter scores +60, even if the planter died in the meantime. 
5. Defuse. If an enemy stays within 1 cell of the planted Core at the end of 2 turns in a row, it is defused. Every enemy next to it scores +40, and the uplink never happens. 
6. Respawn. After an uplink or a defuse, the Core comes back at the centre 4 turns later. 
The Core's position and status are always visible to every bot. 
If two bots finish a map on the same score 
The tie is broken in this order: more kills, then fewer deaths, then more cargo banked, then more resources collected.

Fair play built in 
Your starting corner rotates across the series. 
The groups are drawn in advance. 
If teams are level on rank points 
The tie is broken in this order. The first rule that separates the teams decides. 
1. Head-to-head: how many times each team finished above the other tied teams in this series. 2. Map wins: more maps finished 1st. 
3. Total score across the series. 
4. Core captures. 
5. Banked cargo value. 
If a rule separates some of the tied teams but not all, the ones still level start again from head-to-head among just themselves. The standings screen in the show names the rule and the numbers for every tie.
When a bot breaks 
A bot that crashes, takes too long, or returns a bad action loses that turn, not the match. 
After 3 failures in a row, it is switched off for the rest of that match. 
A broken bot only hurts its own team.

By default, play.py puts your bot against three copies of itself. The tournament uses maps and opponents you have never seen. Use -seeds to see many maps, and -vs to play against other bots, including ones you write yourself. 
The one rule about your code 
decide(view) must return one action within 200 milliseconds. So must buy(shop) . Aim for tens of milliseconds: the tournament machine is busier than your laptop. 
Your bot must do exactly the same thing every time it gets the same input. No time.time() , no unseeded random , no reading files, no internet. A seeded generator like random.Random(7) is fine. 
Your bot keeps one object for the whole match, so self.anything = . remembers things between turns. 
Your class must be called MyBot . Put every helper inside my_bot.py . A second file works on your laptop but does not exist at the tournament. 
Import only vibe_sdk and the Python standard library. Some modules are blocked, like os , sys , subprocess , socket , pickle and threading , and some calls, like eval , exec and open . check.py tells you if you break this.

From sketch to code: The Walker 
Here is The Walker as a complete my_bot.py . It passes check.py and conformance.py . from vibe_sdk import DIRECTION_DELTAS, Action, Bot 
class MyBot(Bot): 
ABILITY = None 
ROLE = None 
def decide(self, view): 
me = view["self"] 
enemies = view["visible_enemies"] 
if enemies: 
target = enemies[0] 
distance = max(abs(target["x"] - me["x"]), abs(target["y"] - me["y"])) 
# Next to an enemy and enough energy: attack it. 
if distance = 1 and me["energy"] = 5: 
return Action.attack(target["id"]) 
# Otherwise walk toward it. 
return Action.move(self.step_toward(me, target["x"], target["y"])) 
# Nobody in sight: walk to the middle. 
board = view["board"] 
return Action.move(self.step_toward(me, board["width"] / 2, board["height"] / 2)) 
def step_toward(self, me, target_x, target_y): 
dx = (target_x > me["x"]) - (target_x < me["x"]) 
dy = (target_y > me["y"]) - (target_y < me["y"]) 
if (dx, dy) = (0, 0): 
dx = 1 if me["x"] = 0 else -1 
for name, delta in DIRECTION_DELTAS.items(): 
if delta = (dx, dy): 
return name 
return "EAST"

The bot walks into a wall every turn. That is legal, so check.py passes. But the bot is stuck. Valid does not mean good. This is why you read the turn log, and then tell your AI what you saw. 
Mistakes that break a bot 
Assuming something is in the view. Fog of war means most lists are often empty. Empty lists and missing things are normal. 
Not checking energy, range, board edges or ability cooldown before returning an action. 
Assuming an item exists. Each match uses only some items. Check view["item_pool"] . Assuming the walls are the same as last time. They change every match. Read view["walls"] . Using slow code. Searching the whole board many times per turn can pass 200 ms. 
Waiting until 10:25 to run conformance.py . 
LET THE ENGINE DO THE GEO M ETRY 
vibe_sdk.geometry is the engine's own wall code: line_clear , shot_path and step_open . If your bot uses it, it always agrees with the engine about sight lines and shot paths.

11 FA Q 
Questions 
Can we use any AI tool? − 
Yes. Any AI tool, free or paid, while you build. Free plans work. After the 10:30 code freeze, your bot runs alone: it cannot call an AI or use the internet during a match. 
Do we need to install anything except Python? − No. You need Python 3.9 or newer. The kit needs no pip install . 
How big is a team? − Two people. 
When do we get the starter kit? − On the day, at 9:00 AM. It is shared as a QR code on screen. 
When do we get our team ID? − At reporting, from 8:45 to 9:00 AM. You need it in your submission email. 
What exactly do we submit? − 
One email before 10:30 AM with your my_bot.py file, your team name and your team ID. The class inside must be called MyBot . 
What if our bot fails the admission test? − 
It is rejected. That is why you run python conformance.py yourself, early. A bot that crashes, takes longer than 200 ms, behaves differently between runs, or makes even one illegal action fails. 
Can we see the other teams' code or scores? − No. During a match your bot sees only its own score and its rank. Nobody sees another team's code. 
Is the result on the day final? − 
No. It is tentative. Final results are announced at the closing ceremony on Saturday 24 October, from 9:00 AM, in the Main Auditorium. 
Is there a best strategy? − 
No single strategy is right. Kills, cargo and the Core all score. How well a plan does depends on the other three bots, and you will not know who they are until the match.
